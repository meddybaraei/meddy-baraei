## 1. FTC Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR 437), the proposed Earnings Claim Rule, and the 2023–2026 AI "make money" enforcement wave

### Takeaway
The single biggest risk is positioning. Marketing "we'll turn your idea into a business that makes money," plus "done-for-you" stores or ads and earnings or ROI promises, is exactly the fact pattern the FTC has sued at least 6 AI/e-commerce "business opportunity" sellers over since 2023. Most ended in lifetime industry bans. The defendants were sued for (a) earnings claims, (b) "AI does it for you / passive" claims, (c) refund or buy-back guarantees they didn't honor, (d) not giving the 437 disclosure document, and (e) gagging customer reviews. The expanded rule proposed in January 2025, which would reach "money-making opportunities" such as business coaching, was **not confirmed finalized** as of Oct 2026.

### Cited Findings
**The rule itself**
- 16 CFR 437 is still in force. Its earnings-claim provision (§437.4) requires the seller to have a reasonable basis for any earnings claim and to have written substantiation in its possession at the time the claim is made. — [eCFR 16 CFR 437](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-437); [NatLawReview summary](https://natlawreview.com/article/ftc-proposes-changes-business-opportunity-rule-deter-deceptive-earnings-claims)
- [background, not re-verified] Trigger test under 437.1: the seller solicits a prospective purchaser to enter a new business; the purchaser makes a required payment; and the seller represents that it will provide "business assistance." That means (i) providing locations for vending machines, rack displays or similar equipment, (ii) providing outlets, accounts **or customers** (including internet outlets, accounts or customers), or (iii) buying back goods or services the purchaser makes or provides. If the rule applies, the seller must give a one-page disclosure document (earnings claims, legal actions, cancellation/refund policy, references) at least **7 calendar days** before the buyer signs or pays. If earnings claims are made, it must also give a separate earnings claim statement. Franchises covered by the Franchise Rule (16 CFR 436) are excluded. — rule text at [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-437); FTC guide [Selling a Work-at-Home or Other Business Opportunity?](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/selling-work-home-or-other-business-opportunity-revised-rule-may-apply-you-1)
- The FTC alleged that Automators broke the Business Opportunity Rule by making income misrepresentations and by failing to give prospective buyers the required disclosure document. — [FTC case page: Automators](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/automators); [Fullerton Observer coverage, Aug 2023](https://fullertonobserver.com/2023/08/22/federal-trade-commission-stops-business-opportunity-scheme-that-promised-its-ai-boosted-tools-would-power-high-earnings-through-online-stores/)

**The proposed expansion (Jan 13, 2025 NPRM)**
- The FTC proposed (1) expanding the Business Opportunity Rule to cover "money-making opportunities" such as business coaching and investment opportunities, (2) a new Earnings Claim Rule (16 CFR 462), and (3) exempting MLMs from 437 so the new earnings rule covers them instead. The changes would let the FTC get consumer redress and civil penalties for deceptive earnings claims. — [FTC NPRM PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/r111003earningclaimsnprm01132025.pdf); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/ftc-proposes-changes-business-opportunity-rule-deter-deceptive-earnings-claims); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/consumer-trading-unfair-trading/1571462/ftc-takes-aim-at-deceptive-earnings-claims); [Franchise Memorandum](https://www.thefranchisememorandum.com/ftc-proposes-changes-to-deter-deceptive-earnings-claims-by-multilevel-marketers-and-money-making-opportunity-sellers/)
- Status: two separate searches found no final rule, withdrawal or hearing notice through Oct 2026. A tracker site (mmoldatabase.com) says it covers the September 2026 status, but it could not be fetched. — [mmoldatabase](https://mmoldatabase.com/the-rules.html) (unverified)

**Enforcement cases: what got them sued**
- **Automators AI (filed Aug 2023; settled Feb 2024):** The FTC alleged the company used false claims that AI-boosted tools would power high earnings from online stores, causing more than $22M in consumer harm. Counts: FTC Act §5, the Business Opportunity Rule (earnings misrepresentations, no disclosure document) and the Consumer Review Fairness Act. Defendants Roman Cresto, John Cresto and Andrew Chapman got a lifetime ban on selling e-commerce business opportunities or coaching and surrendered assets (about $21.7M judgment, largely suspended). — [FTC: Automators](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/automators); [Verdict](https://www.verdict.co.uk/newsletters/ftc-pursues-lifetime-e-commerce-ban-for-ai-scheme-owners); [regulations.ai entry](https://regulations.ai/enforcement/enf-us-automatorsaiandr-20240227)
- **FBA Machine / Passive Scaling (filed June 2024):** Sold "guaranteed income" turnkey e-commerce stores and took in more than $15.9M. It ran as Passive Scaling and rebranded to FBA Machine in 2023 after refund demands. Counts: §5, the Business Opportunity Rule and the CRFA. The final order permanently bars the operators from selling business opportunities and from contract terms that stop consumers leaving honest reviews. — [ABA Antitrust newsletter](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/antitrust_law/resources/newsletters/ftc-continues-crackdown-ai-deceit-e-commerce/); [FKKS Advertising Law](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102kymz/ftc-shuts-down-ai-driven-business-opportunity-scheme-continues-sweep-of-deceptiv)
- **Ascend Ecom and Ecommerce Empire Builders (Sept 2024, part of "Operation AI Comply"):** Ascend claimed its AI tools would help consumers earn thousands of dollars a month from stores on Amazon, Walmart, Etsy and TikTok. Customers paid tens of thousands upfront plus inventory, and the FTC alleged at least $25M in losses. Ecommerce Empire Builders promised up to $10,000 a month from "AI-powered" stores. — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20241015-the-ftc-cracks-down-on-unfair-and-deceptive-practices-involving-the-use-of-ai); [Lina Khan on X](https://x.com/linamkhan/status/1839782815501598862); [RetailWire](https://retailwire.com/ftcs-operation-ai-comply-accuses-ascend-ecom-of-misleading-users/); [EPIC](https://epic.org/ftc-takes-action-against-businesses-using-unproven-deceptive-and-fraudulent-ai-systems/)
- **Click Profit (filed March 2025; proposed settlement Aug 2025):** The FTC alleged false claims of an AI-driven "passive income" system that would build fully operational stores on Amazon, Walmart and TikTok, plus false affiliations with Nike and Disney. Losses were at least $14M. The proposed orders bar the defendants from business-opportunity sales, from false earnings, affiliation or AI claims, and from restricting consumers' truthful reviews. — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/33007-federal-trade-commission-issued-proposed-orders-against-click-profit-for-alleged-misrepresentation-of-online-sales-opportunities); [AI Fraud Tracker](https://aifraudtracker.com/entry/click-profit/); [FKKS](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102kymz/ftc-shuts-down-ai-driven-business-opportunity-scheme-continues-sweep-of-deceptiv)
- **Air AI (filed Aug 2025, D. Ariz.; settled March 24, 2026):** This case is most analogous to a B2B "AI that grows your business" platform. The FTC said Air AI claimed its conversational AI could replace customer service reps and, with other services, let buyers earn back tens of thousands of dollars within days or months, and some could make millions. It promised refunds or "buy-back" if buyers didn't earn 2–3x their investment, and those promises were rarely honored. Some small businesses lost up to $250,000 and were left in debt. Counts included §5, the **Telemarketing Sales Rule** and the **Business Opportunity Rule** (including failing to provide disclosure documents). Settlement: ban on marketing business opportunities, an $18M judgment largely suspended, and $50,000 for consumer relief, subject to court approval. — [FTC press release, Aug 2025](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-sues-stop-air-ai-using-deceptive-claims-about-business-growth-earnings-potential-refund); [FTC press release, Mar 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/air-ai-its-owners-will-be-banned-marketing-business-opportunities-settle-ftc-charges-company-misled); [FTC case page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/airai); [CFO Dive](https://www.cfodive.com/news/ai-startup-reaped-millions-using-bogus-claims-ftc-suit/758587/); [PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/news/regulation/2026/ftc-seeks-to-ban-air-ai-from-marketing-business-opportunities)
- **Ecom Genie / Profitable Automation:** Permanent bans and judgments totaling nearly $14M. — summarized from FTC releases via search; see [FTC AI page](https://www.ftc.gov/industry/technology/artificial-intelligence)
- Common pattern across the cases: bold income guarantees, inflated testimonials, and marketing built around "automated" or "AI-driven" systems. — [FKKS](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102kymz/ftc-shuts-down-ai-driven-business-opportunity-scheme-continues-sweep-of-deceptiv)

### Inferences
- **When this platform triggers 437:** If it promises to provide customers for the client's new business (e.g., "we run ads that bring you customers/leads/sales"), sells to people *starting a new business*, and requires payment, it plausibly meets the "outlets, accounts or customers" prong. A pure software or marketing-services vendor that sells to existing businesses and makes no earnings or customer-delivery promises is much less likely to be covered. The product promise "turn your idea into a business that makes money" leans toward coverage.
- Air AI shows the FTC will apply 437 and the TSR to B2B and SMB buyers, not only "consumers." Selling to small businesses is not a safe harbor.
- If the 2025 NPRM is finalized, "business coaching / money-making opportunities" coverage would likely capture this model even without a "customers" promise. Building 437-style disclosures now is cheap insurance.
- Highest-risk claims to avoid: "$X/month," "passive income," "AI does everything," "guaranteed ROI / money back if you don't make 2x," celebrity or brand affiliations, cherry-picked testimonials, and non-disparagement clauses.

### Gaps
- Whether the January 2025 Earnings Claim Rule / BOR amendments were finalized, withdrawn or stalled by Oct 2026 could not be confirmed. Check the Federal Register and ftc.gov rulemaking pages.
- Final court approval status of the Air AI and Click Profit settlements was not confirmed.
- The 437 trigger elements and the 7-day rule above are from background knowledge, because the eCFR page was not fetched this session.

## 2. State business opportunity laws (Virginia) and franchise-law triggers

### Takeaway
Virginia's Business Opportunity Sales Act (Va. Code §59.1-262 et seq.) has a low trigger: more than $500 paid, plus a representation such as "we'll provide a sales or marketing program that enables you to earn more than you paid." That maps closely onto this platform's pitch. If triggered, it requires a written disclosure statement, a bond or escrow in some cases, and a written contract, and it gives buyers private remedies. Franchise law is a secondary risk, triggered only if the platform licenses its own brand to clients and prescribes the marketing plan.

### Cited Findings
- §59.1-263 defines "business opportunity" as the sale of products, equipment, supplies or services where the purchaser pays **more than $500** to start a business, and the seller makes certain representations. The current text lists the representation that the seller will provide a sales or marketing program that will enable the purchaser to derive income exceeding the price paid. Earlier versions also list location-finding, buy-back and income-guarantee prongs. Exclusions include securities, franchises, certain retailer trademark licenses and newspaper distribution. — [Va. Code §59.1-263](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/59.1-263); [Chapter 21 full text](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title59.1/chapter21/); [vacode.org 2025](https://vacode.org/2025/59.1/21/)
- Chapter 21 also requires a written disclosure statement (§59.1-264), bond or escrow (§59.1-265), prohibited acts (§59.1-266), written contracts (§59.1-267), purchaser remedies (§59.1-268) and penalties (§59.1-269). — [LIS popular name: Business Opportunity Sales Act](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodepopularnames/business-opportunity-sales-act/)
- Other states (e.g., California, Nebraska) regulate "seller assisted marketing plans" with their own registration and disclosure regimes. — [Nebraska SAMP materials](https://ndbf.nebraska.gov/node/106); [CA Civ. Code 1812.201](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1812-201/)
- [background, not re-verified] Under the federal Franchise Rule (16 CFR 436), a franchise exists when (1) the buyer operates under the seller's trademark, (2) the seller exerts significant control or gives significant assistance, and (3) the buyer pays at least the minimum payment threshold within the first 6 months. Virginia has its own Retail Franchising Act (Va. Code §13.1-557 et seq.) with registration.

### Inferences
- Because Virginia's statute applies wherever there is a >$500 payment plus a "marketing program will let you earn more than you paid" representation, the platform's core tagline could on its own satisfy the representation element for Virginia buyers. Use plain-language "no income representation" positioning, avoid "earn back your investment," and keep a Virginia-compliant disclosure statement ready, or have counsel confirm an exemption.
- Franchise risk is low if clients build their *own* brands. It rises if the platform offers a branded "network" (e.g., "[Platform] Home Services Partner") with required operating methods.

### Gaps
- The full current text of §59.1-263 to §59.1-269 (bond amounts, filing with the Virginia Division of Consumer Counsel or SCC, cancellation rights) was not fetched because the LIS site was unreachable.
- No Virginia AG enforcement actions under the Business Opportunity Sales Act against AI or marketing platforms were found.

## 3. Truth-in-advertising for ads generated for clients (FTC Act §5, Endorsement Guides, Consumer Reviews Rule, AI content, liability for clients' claims)

### Takeaway
The platform can be directly liable as an ad agency or "means and instrumentalities" provider for deceptive claims in the ads and sites it generates. The 2024 Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR 465) carries civil penalties of about $53K per violation, and the FTC began warning-letter enforcement in Dec 2025. An AI tool that auto-writes testimonials or reviews is a red flag.

### Cited Findings
- The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (final Aug 2024, effective Oct 2024) bans fake reviews and testimonials (including AI-generated ones attributed to people who don't exist), incentives conditioned on sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews, review suppression, company-controlled "independent" review sites, and fake social-media indicators. — [Crowell](https://crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/final-rule-announced-the-ftc-strengthens-its-enforcement-capacity-against-deceptive-reviews-and-testimonials); [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2024/08/20/ftc-issues-final-rule-to-fight-fake-product-reviews/)
- On Dec 22, 2025, the FTC sent warning letters to 10 companies under the rule and required written corrective steps within 5 days. Penalties are up to $53,088 per violation (2026 inflation-adjusted figure). Liability attaches where the business "knew or should have known" content was fake. — [Venable, Dec 2025](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-signals-heightened-enforcement-of-new); [Arnold & Porter, Jan 2026](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/consumer-products-and-retail-navigator/2026/01/ftc-warning-letters-over-consumer-review-rule); [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-enforcement-under-consumer-review-rule/); [KJK, Aug 2026](https://kjk.com/2026/08/06/ftc-consumer-review-rule-enforcement-what-businesses-need-to-know/); [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2026/01/07/ftc-signals-crackdown-on-fake-reviews-and-testimonials)
- No filed lawsuits under the rule were found as of the sources reviewed. One April 2026 alert says the FTC is targeting company-wide practices. — [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-enforcement-under-consumer-review-rule/)
- Several of the business-opportunity cases also charged CRFA violations for contract terms that stopped customers from posting honest reviews. — [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/antitrust_law/resources/newsletters/ftc-continues-crackdown-ai-deceit-e-commerce/)
- [background, not re-verified] The FTC's 2023 revised Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255) require clear and conspicuous disclosure of material connections. They say "results not typical" disclaimers do not cure atypical testimonials, cover virtual or AI influencers, and say advertisers are responsible for misleading endorser claims. Ad agencies and service providers can be liable under §5 when they participate in creating deceptive ads ("knew or should have known").

### Inferences
- Product design controls: (1) block generation of fake reviews, testimonials, "as seen on," invented stats, health or earnings claims; (2) require the client to attest to substantiation for objective claims (prices, "licensed & insured," "#1," results); (3) keep logs of who approved each ad. Logs help with "knew or should have known" defenses.
- There is no general federal AI-content-disclosure law for ads as of these sources. The risk is deception-based (e.g., AI-generated "customer photos" or people shown as real customers).

### Gaps
- No primary-source verification this session of the 2023 Endorsement Guides text or of FTC statements on AI-generated ad disclosure.
- No case law was found on marketing-platform liability for SMB clients' ad claims specifically.

## 4. Ad-platform policies that commonly get SMB accounts suspended

### Takeaway
Account suspensions are an operational and legal risk. In the US, Google requires Advanced Verification for locksmiths and garage-door services. Meta requires "Special Ad Category" designation (with targeting limits) for financial products and services (from Jan 14, 2025), housing, employment and credit. Mis-categorization leads to rejection. Google's misrepresentation policy is the most common SMB suspension cause [background].

### Cited Findings
- Google: "all locksmith businesses in the US and Canada, and garage door services advertising in the United States must pass Advanced Verification." The process includes checks of license validity and business registration, fraud-history screening, and reportedly an owner video interview. It can take 2–3 months per third-party guides. Plumbers are listed as restricted only in the Netherlands in the search summary. — [Google: About Advanced Verification](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7167635); [Google Ads policy: Local services](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/13527633); [LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/search-marketing-listings-management/google-advanced-verification-locksmiths-and-garage-door/); [Marketing 360](https://help.marketing360.com/portal/en/kb/articles/googleadsadvancedverificationforlocksmithservicesandgaragedoorservices6865776602954)
- A third-party blog reports a "Google Verified" badge requirement for Local Services Ads starting Oct 20, 2025. This was not confirmed from a Google source. — [Nebula Design](https://nebula.design/?p=15672)
- Meta: from Jan 14, 2025, US advertisers (or ads shown to US audiences) must use the "Financial products and services" special ad category, which replaces "Credit." The housing and employment targeting restrictions apply: no age, gender or ZIP targeting, no exclusion targeting, and no lookalikes. Ads may be rejected if the right category isn't chosen. A planned restriction on customer-list custom audiences for these categories (March 2025) was later delayed. — [Meta Marketing API: Special Ad Category](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/special-ad-category); [Meta 2024 out-of-cycle changes](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/out-of-cycle-changes/occ-2024); [Data Axle](https://www.data-axle.com/resources/blog/meta-special-ad-categories-rules/)
- [background, not re-verified] Google's Misrepresentation policy (including "unreliable claims," "dishonest pricing," "unavailable offers," and get-rich-quick "Business opportunities" content) and Google's healthcare and financial services certification requirements are frequent causes of SMB disapprovals and suspensions. Suspensions for "circumventing systems" can cascade to linked accounts, which matters for an agency that runs many client accounts under one manager (MCC).

### Inferences
- An agency platform running many client accounts from one manager account creates contagion risk: one bad client can trigger linked-account suspensions. Use separate, client-owned ad accounts and payment methods.
- Build vertical gating: detect locksmith, garage door, financial, health, housing, employment or legal ideas, then route to the right verification or category before launch.
- Ironically, the platform's *own* ads ("make money with AI") are likely to hit Google's and Meta's business-opportunity and get-rich-quick restrictions.

### Gaps
- Google policy pages could not be fetched. The full list of US local-service categories that need Advanced Verification, and Google's 2025–2026 business-operations verification changes, should be confirmed directly.
- Meta's current (2026) state of the CLCA restriction was not confirmed.

## 5. Contractor licensing (Virginia DPOR), TCPA / one-to-one consent, CAN-SPAM, VCDPA, and pixel/tracking lawsuits

### Takeaway
(a) Generating leads for unlicensed Virginia contractors on jobs of $1,000 or more facilitates a Class 1 misdemeanor. Verify DPOR licenses at onboarding for home-services clients. (b) The FCC one-to-one consent rule was vacated in Jan 2025 and never took effect. Ordinary TCPA prior-express-written-consent rules still apply to marketing texts and autodialed or AI-voice calls. (c) The VCDPA has no private right of action, but CIPA wiretap suits over pixels are a large private-litigation wave, mostly in California, that can reach any site with California visitors.

### Cited Findings
**Virginia contractor licensing**
- A contractor license is required before bidding or doing contracting work of $1,000 or more. Class C covers single projects over $1,000 but under $30,000 (or 12-month volume under $250,000), and higher classes follow. Unlicensed contracting under §54.1-1115 is a Class 1 misdemeanor with civil fines, and contracts may be unenforceable by the unlicensed contractor. — [Va. Code Title 54.1 Ch. 11](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title54.1/chapter11/); [Culpeper County](https://web.culpepercounty.gov/building/page/contractors-licensing); [Greenspun Law](https://www.greenspunlaw.com/blog/legal-help-for-unpaid-contractors-fairfax-virginia.cfm); [exam-prep summary of class thresholds](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/va-general-contractor/ch1/ch1-sec2)
- Sources conflict on class dollar ranges: older county pages show Class C $1,000–$10,000. Use current DPOR materials. — [Culpeper County](https://web.culpepercounty.gov/node/2328) vs. [Va. Code Ch. 11](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title54.1/chapter11/)

**TCPA**
- Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC (11th Cir., Jan 24, 2025) vacated the FCC's "one-to-one consent" and "logically and topically related" requirements and remanded. The court held that common-law consent lets a consumer consent to multiple sellers at once. The rule had been delayed to Jan 26, 2026 and never took effect. — [Morrison Foerster](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/01/eleventh-circuit-overrules-fccs-one-to-one); [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/one-to-none-the-fcc-and-eleventh-circuit-issue-last-minute-rulings-gutting-the-one-to-one-tcpa-consent-rule.html); [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2025-01-31-telephone-and-texting-compliance-news-litigation-update)
- Air AI's complaint included Telemarketing Sales Rule counts. This is relevant if the platform sells via outbound calls or uses AI voice agents for clients. — [FTC Air AI release](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-sues-stop-air-ai-using-deceptive-claims-about-business-growth-earnings-potential-refund)
- [background, not re-verified] TCPA still requires prior express *written* consent for telemarketing texts and calls made with an autodialer or artificial or prerecorded voice. The FCC's Feb 2024 ruling treats AI-generated voices as "artificial." Statutory damages are $500–$1,500 per message. Carrier 10DLC registration is required for A2P SMS. The Virginia Telephone Privacy Protection Act (Va. Code §59.1-510 et seq.) adds a state private right of action.

**CAN-SPAM** — [background, not re-verified] Commercial email needs an accurate header and subject, a physical address, a working opt-out honored within 10 business days, and an ad identification. Both the sender and the business being promoted can be liable. Per-email civil penalties are about $53K (inflation-adjusted).

**VCDPA / privacy**
- The VCDPA gives opt-out rights for targeted advertising, sale and profiling. The AG has exclusive enforcement (no private right of action), with civil penalties up to $7,500 per violation. "Targeted advertising" excludes ads based on activity within the controller's own sites. 2025 amendments added protections for children under 13. A 2026 precise-geolocation sale ban (SB 338, effective July 1, 2026) is reported by secondary sources. — [Virginia AG VCDPA summary (7/1/26)](https://www.oag.state.va.us/consumer-protection/files/tips-and-info/Virginia-Consumer-Data-Protection-Act-Summary-7-1-26.pdf); [Privacy Rights Clearinghouse](https://privacyrights.org/resources-tools/law-overviews/virginia-consumer-data-protection-act); [privacylawmap (lower reliability)](https://privacylawmap.com/states/virginia)
- [background, not re-verified] VCDPA applicability thresholds (100,000 Virginia consumers, or 25,000 plus more than 50% of revenue from data sales) mean most SMB clients are below them, but the platform itself may cross them in aggregate as a processor or controller.

**Pixel/tracking litigation**
- More than 4,000 digital wiretapping (CIPA §631 and pen-register §638.51) suits have been filed since 2022, over 75% in California, with about 800 in 2025 and no slowdown into 2026. Statutory damages are $5,000 per violation. Consent banners help only if tracking doesn't fire before consent. — [Concord](https://www.concord.tech/blog/california-wiretapping-claims-website-tracking); [CookieScript 2026 roundup](https://cookie-script.com/news/consumer-privacy-lawsuit-roundup-2026-from-cipa-to-coppa); [Metaverse Law](https://www.metaverselaw.com/california-invasion-cipa-privacy-litigation-trend/) (mostly vendor sources; counts are estimates)

### Inferences
- The platform should check DPOR licenses (and county or trade licenses such as electrical, plumbing and HVAC tradesman cards) for Virginia home-services clients before launching ads. It should refuse to state "licensed" otherwise. Google Advanced Verification will independently block unlicensed locksmiths and garage-door operators.
- Even with one-to-one vacated, lead forms that feed SMS or AI-voice follow-up should capture clear, seller-specific written consent and keep logs (e.g., TrustedForm or Jornaya-style certificates). Lead buyers and sellers will still demand it.
- Default site templates should load the Meta Pixel, GA and session replay only after consent (consent mode), and include a privacy policy and opt-out link.

### Gaps
- Exact current §54.1-1115 penalty amounts and DPOR class thresholds should be verified on LIS. The pages were unreachable.
- The FCC's remand action on one-to-one consent and the status of the FCC consent-revocation rule ("revoke by any reasonable means," partially delayed) in 2026 were not confirmed.
- No VCDPA-specific pixel lawsuits were found, and none would be expected given there is no private right of action.

## 6. Liability for the platform's AI outputs (trademark, copyright, Section 230)

### Takeaway
AI-generated business names and logos can infringe existing trademarks. Purely AI-generated content is not copyrightable (Thaler, cert denied March 2026), so clients may not "own" their AI logos or copy in an enforceable way. Section 230 is unlikely to shield content the platform itself generates.

### Cited Findings
- The Supreme Court denied cert in Thaler v. Perlmutter on March 2, 2026 (No. 25-449). That leaves the D.C. Circuit's holding that copyrightable works require a human author, while noting that AI-*assisted* works with sufficient human contribution can be protected. How much human involvement is enough remains unresolved. — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-authorship-case); [Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/supreme-court-denies-certiorari-in-thaler-v-perlmutter-ai-cannot-be-an-author-under-the-copyright-act); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102mlpl/supreme-court-denies-certiorari-in-thaler-v-perlmutter-human-only-rule-for-ai/)
- Section 230 protects platforms against liability for third-party content. Intermediaries remain responsible for content they generate "in whole or in part." The provision's authors (Wyden and Cox) argued it does not protect generative AI outputs. No definitive court ruling has resolved the question. — [CDT legal analysis](https://cdt.org/insights/section-230-and-its-applicability-to-generative-ai-a-legal-analysis/); [Mondaq / Crowell, "The Pipe Not the Posts"](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/media-entertainment-law/1825918/the-pipe-not-the-posts-how-section-230s-protections-extend-to-generative-ai-platforms); [Fortune, Oct 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/10/03/ai-chatbot-section-230-meta-social-media-legal-shield-no-protection); [UChicago Business Law Review](https://businesslawreview.uchicago.edu/print-archive/generative-ai-meets-section-230-future-liability-and-its-implications-startup)
- Click Profit was barred from false AI and affiliation claims, including falsely claiming ties to Nike and Disney. This shows brand-affiliation misuse is an enforcement target. — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/33007-federal-trade-commission-issued-proposed-orders-against-click-profit-for-alleged-misrepresentation-of-online-sales-opportunities)

### Inferences
- Run USPTO (TESS successor), state registration and domain or social-handle knockout searches on every generated name. Run similarity checks on logos. Disclaim clearance and recommend an attorney search before filing.
- Tell clients that AI-only logos and copy may not be copyrightable. Offer human designer refinement as a premium tier to strengthen ownership.
- Treat all ad and site copy the platform generates as the platform's own speech for risk purposes, and don't rely on Section 230.

### Gaps
- No case law was found on trademark liability of AI naming or logo generators specifically.
- Pending AI-training copyright suits (e.g., against model providers) and indemnities offered by model vendors were not researched.

## 7. Contract/ToS mitigations and insurance

### Takeaway
The legal design pattern: sell a *service* (websites, ad management, marketing) to clients, with no earnings, revenue or customer-volume representations; client-owned ad accounts and ad spend; transparent, honored refund terms; no review gags; 437 and Virginia BOSA-style disclosures if any trigger risk remains; and E&O, media liability and cyber insurance.

### Cited Findings
- Refund guarantees that weren't honored were a standalone deception count in Air AI ("refund or buy-back if you don't earn 2–3x"). — [FTC Air AI release](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-sues-stop-air-ai-using-deceptive-claims-about-business-growth-earnings-potential-refund)
- Orders in FBA Machine and Click Profit ban contract terms restricting customers' truthful reviews. These terms also violate the CRFA. — [FKKS](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102kymz/ftc-shuts-down-ai-driven-business-opportunity-scheme-continues-sweep-of-deceptiv); [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/antitrust_law/resources/newsletters/ftc-continues-crackdown-ai-deceit-e-commerce/)
- Earnings claims require a reasonable basis and written substantiation at the time made (§437.4). Disclaimers don't cure an unsubstantiated claim. — [eCFR 437](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-437)
- Virginia BOSA requires written disclosures and written contracts for covered sales, and gives purchasers remedies. — [LIS Business Opportunity Sales Act](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodepopularnames/business-opportunity-sales-act/)

### Inferences (recommended mitigations; not legal advice)
1. **Marketing copy:** Drop "makes money." Use "we build and run your website, ads and marketing." Ban specific earnings, ROI or "passive income" claims. If any results are shown, use typical-results data with written substantiation.
2. **Contracts:** Explicit no-income or no-results guarantee (not a substitute for avoiding claims). Client represents it is licensed and that its claims are substantiated, and indemnifies the platform for its own business claims. Platform's limited indemnity and liability cap. AI-output disclaimers. Customer must approve before launch.
3. **Ad spend:** Client-owned Google and Meta accounts and payment methods. Management fee kept separate from media spend. Clear data and account ownership on termination.
4. **Refunds:** Simple, honored, written refund or cancellation policy. Avoid conditional "money back if you don't make X" offers.
5. **Disclosures:** Prepare a 437 disclosure document (7-day wait) and a Virginia BOSA disclosure, or get a written opinion from counsel that the offering is exempt.
6. **Reviews:** No non-disparagement clauses. No incentivized or AI-fabricated testimonials for the platform's own marketing.
7. **Sales process:** Avoid outbound telemarketing scripts with earnings claims (TSR exposure per Air AI). Watch financing or "credit stacking" upsells, a pattern in these cases [background].
8. **Insurance:** Tech E&O plus media and advertising liability (IP or defamation in generated content), cyber or privacy (pixels, CIPA), and possibly D&O. Confirm that exclusions for "regulatory fines" and "AI-generated content" don't gut coverage.

### Gaps
- No sources were found on insurer appetite, pricing, or AI-content exclusions for E&O and media liability in 2026.
- Clauses were not benchmarked against peer ToS (e.g., agency or website-builder platforms).
