# Sell booked jobs, not business dreams

Meddy should not build the general "type an idea, get a money-making business" platform the pitch describes. As of October 2026 every layer of that stack is commoditized or free. Prompt-to-website costs $0–$50/mo. App generation is a $1B+ ARR category led by well-funded players. Google, Meta and TikTok now give away AI ad creative. And the "turn your idea into a business that makes money" promise is the exact pattern behind at least six FTC business-opportunity cases since 2023, most of which ended in lifetime industry bans. What no one does well is the handoff layer: one closed loop from brand to site, ads, call/lead capture, follow-up and booked job, with conversion data sent back to the ad platforms. That loop is worth the most in home services, where a $76–$90 lead can turn into a $1,200–$3,200 job. The evidence points to a narrower, more defensible business. Productize the NovaProHome playbook as a "launch and run" system for licensed tradespeople and small home-services operators in Northern Virginia. Run it as a tech-enabled service first, keep a free AI brand-and-site generator as the top of the funnel, put ads on customer-owned Google and Meta accounts, and position it legally as a marketing service with no income promises. The broad idea-to-business vision can stay the long-term story. It should not be the first product, the tagline or the pricing model.

## Every layer is crowded; only the handoffs are empty

The builder layer is already won on price. Durable gives a full AI site free on a subdomain and charges about $15–$95/mo for a custom domain and business tools ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/software-services/durable); [dailyaifixs](https://dailyaifixs.com/blog/durable-pricing-2026-free-is-just-a-subdomain)). GoDaddy's Airo, launched in beta in November 2025, takes a user **from a business idea through domain registration, site, logo and a hosted app** using six coordinated agents. That is the "idea to business" story in nearly the words of Meddy's pitch, sold by the company that owns the domain-registration moment ([MarketScreener / GoDaddy PR](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/godaddy-brings-agentic-ai-to-small-businesses-with-launch-of-airo-ai-ce7d5fdddf88f021)). Wix's AI builder makes a full site from a Q&A, and its acquisition of Base44 reached about **$150M ARR by May 2026** ([Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/wix-stock-plunges-27-on-q1-return-to-losses-despite-base44-ai-platform-hitting-150m-arr-ahead-of-internal-targets/)).

The software layer is even more crowded. Lovable reached **$400M ARR in February 2026** ([TechDogs](https://www.techdogs.com/tech-news/td-newsdesk/lovable-hits-400m-arr-in-february-adds-100m-in-a-month-with-146-employees)), and Replit reached about **$525M annualized by April 2026** after raising at a $9B valuation ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/replit-passes-500m-year/)). A solo founder will not out-build these companies on "build the software."

The cautionary note is margin. Wix's Q1 2026 results showed AI generation driving growth while compute costs and Base44 spending pushed the company back to a **$57.5M net loss, and its shares fell about 27%** ([Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/wix-stock-plunges-27-on-q1-return-to-losses-despite-base44-ai-platform-hitting-150m-arr-ahead-of-internal-targets/)). Generating software is a cost center, not a moat.

On ads, the platforms are absorbing the work themselves. Meta has said publicly that it wants a business to supply only a product image and a budget, with AI producing creative, targeting and budget allocation by the end of 2026 ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/meta-aims-to-fully-automate-advertising-with-ai-by-2026-144037.html)). In 2026 that system is reported only as being tested with select advertisers, not generally available ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/meta-ai-automated-ads-2026-marketing-guide)). Google's AI Max for Search is out of beta ([Google](https://business.google.com/us/accelerate/announcements/ai-max-for-search-campaigns/)). TikTok's Symphony suite generates scripts, avatar videos and dubbing, and automatically shifts budget to winning assets ([Vidjet](https://www.vidjet.com/blog/latest-features-on-tiktok-symphony-studio-july-2026-update)). Third-party creative tools at $14–$99/mo are being squeezed between free platform tools and enterprise products like Smartly, which charges a percent of spend with practical minimums of about $4–5K/mo ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/guides/best-ai-ad-creative-tools); [Superscale](https://superscale.ai/alternatives/smartly)). So "we launch your ads with AI" is becoming a thin wrapper around free platform features.

The direct "AI runs your company" startups are young and contested. Polsia charges **$49/mo plus a 20% revenue share** and reportedly raised at a $250M valuation, but its revenue claims are self-reported, and one modeled estimate puts real revenue at a small fraction of the claims ([Henry Shi](https://henrythe9th.substack.com/p/how-a-solo-founder-cloned-himself); [StartupHub](https://www.startuphub.ai/startups/polsia-inc)). Marblism grew its "AI engineer" to $250K ARR, then killed it and pivoted ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/HPkokYNZWrMX8GCaSWAd)). Both suggest that selling automation without measurable revenue does not retain customers.

Meanwhile, the incumbents in local lead generation leave contractors angry. Angi reviewers describe leads that rarely answer and are shared among many bidders ([Kimola analysis](https://kimola.com/reports/unlock-insights-angi-leads-customer-feedback-analysis-trustpilot-en-us-149084)). Shared marketplace leads reportedly close at only **6–10% versus 18–24% for organic search leads** ([automatethejourney](https://automatethejourney.com/firstlap/blog/are-angi-thumbtack-leads-worth-it)).

GoHighLevel is the de facto backend for "agency-in-a-box" local marketing ($97–$497/mo, white-label resale) ([Apexure](https://www.apexure.com/blog/gohighlevel-pricing)). It does not buy media, and users complain about stacked usage fees and A2P 10DLC texting registration ([Ecosire](https://ecosire.com/blog/gohighlevel-pricing-worth-it-2026)).

Put together, each layer has a strong owner: GoDaddy and Wix for sites, Lovable and Replit for apps, the ad platforms for creative and targeting, GHL for CRM and SMS, Angi and Thumbtack for leads. But no product carries a local service business from brand to site to paid ads to call tracking to speed-to-lead to booked job, and then feeds that outcome back into ad optimization. That gap is the opening. It is also exactly what Meddy already did by hand for NovaProHome.

## Ad platform APIs are obtainable, but account ownership shapes the product

Technical access is not the barrier it was a year ago.

**Google.** Google retired developer tokens on September 9, 2026. API access levels now attach to the Google Cloud project. New Basic and Standard applications require brand verification ([Google: Developer token](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/api-policy/developer-token)). After brand verification, **Basic access (15,000 operations per day) is now approved automatically within minutes**, while Standard still gets a manual review ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/google-drops-developer-tokens-from-ads-api-access-decisions/); [Google: Access levels](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/access-levels?hl=en)). The lighter Explorer tier reaches production accounts but is capped at 2,880 operations per day, and it **blocks creating client accounts and billing services**, so a multi-customer platform needs Basic at minimum ([Google Ads Developer Blog](https://ads-developers.googleblog.com/2025/10/explorer-access-is-now-available-for.html?m=1)). Once a tool reaches Standard, Google's Required Minimum Functionality applies: a full-service tool used by third parties must offer creation, management and reporting. Any change in how the API is used requires a Tool Change Form ([Google: RMF](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/api-policy/rmf)). Search, Performance Max, Demand Gen and AI Max are all well supported through the API ([Google Ads Developer Blog 2026](https://ads-developers.googleblog.com/2026/?m=0)), and Demand Gen and PMax are the practical API route to YouTube inventory.

**Google Local Services Ads.** This is a key gap for home services. **The API cannot create Local Services campaigns.** It can only change status, budget, bidding, schedule and service types, rate leads and pull lead reports ([Google: Local Services campaigns](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/campaigns/local-service-campaigns?authuser=9)). LSA onboarding (profile, background check, license check) stays a hands-on step, which is one reason a service layer is unavoidable early.

**Meta.** Managing other businesses' ad accounts needs App Review approval for `ads_management`, Full Access (renamed from "Advanced" in May 2026) and Business Verification. The Full Access threshold dropped to **500 API calls in 15 days** ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.meta.com/blog/updates-to-ads-management-standard-access-feature/); [admanage.ai](https://admanage.ai/blog/meta-ads-api)). The binding constraint is not the API but account trust. New ad accounts commonly start with spending limits of **about $50 per day**, and there is no easy way to request an increase ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/campaign-spending-limits-account-spending-limits-daily-spending-limits/)). Every API campaign must also declare a special ad category. Financial products and services has been mandatory for US audiences since January 2025, and it removes age, gender, ZIP and lookalike targeting ([Meta: Special Ad Category](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/marketing-api/audiences/special-ad-category)). A platform that spins up batches of brand-new Pages, ad accounts and AI creative matches the profile that triggers Meta integrity reviews. Spend ramps have to be slow and honest.

**TikTok.** TikTok's US risk is largely settled. The TikTok USDS Joint Venture was established on January 22, 2026, and eMarketer forecasts **27.9% US TikTok ad revenue growth in 2026** ([Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/tiktok-pitches-advertisers-on-bold-new-chapter-under-us-joint-venture/815632/); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/tiktok-sponsored-content-rebounds-quickly-after-us-deal--with-volume-up-17)). Production Marketing API access still requires sandbox development, app review and a data-security check, so plan for a multi-week approval ([unified.to](https://unified.to/blog/how_to_get_your_tiktok_ads_api_key)).

**Other platforms.** Microsoft Advertising is the cheapest add-on: agency approval takes up to five business days, and Google campaigns can be mirrored to it ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/advertising/guides/get-started)). LinkedIn takes **four weeks at best and four months on average** to approve, and is irrelevant to local trades ([Microsoft Learn: LinkedIn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/increasing-access); [Phyllo](https://www.getphyllo.com/post/linkedin-api-access-in-2026-partner-program-approval-timeline-alternatives)). Nextdoor's Ads API is partner-request based, comes with a Conversions API, and is a natural hyperlocal fit ([Nextdoor](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/nextdoor-launches-ads-api-program-offering-advertisers-an-easier-way-to-extend-their-campaigns-to-nextdoor)).

**Google Business Profile.** The GBP API stays at **0 queries per minute until a manual review approves it**. Google says reviews take up to 14 days, and developers report longer silences in 2026 ([Google: GBP FAQ](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/faq); [Google Developer forum](https://discuss.google.dev/t/gbp-api-access-request-no-response-quota-still-0-qpm-after-resubmission/380564)). Apply on day one.

**Billing.** Billing decides the account architecture. Google consolidated billing and Meta monthly invoicing are both credit lines aimed at established agencies ([Google: Consolidated billing](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375371); [aimerce](https://www.aimerce.ai/blogs/what-is-meta-ads-monthly-invoicing)), and a startup will not qualify. Fronting media spend would expose the platform to card chargebacks on spend that has already run and cannot be refunded, and one fraudulent customer could taint the shared manager account. The architecture is therefore clear. **Each customer owns its Google Ads account, Meta Business Portfolio and card on file.** The platform holds a manager account (MCC) and partner access, and charges only its own fee through Stripe.

**Build vs. buy.** Unified-API vendors such as Unified.to cover reporting and simple writes (pause, budget) across many networks ([Unified.to](https://unified.to/blog/best_unified_api_for_advertising_in_2026)). Google and Meta still need native integrations to handle PMax/Advantage+ structure, special categories and RMF.

## The tagline is the single largest legal risk

The FTC has sued a string of sellers whose pitch overlaps with "turn your idea into a business that makes money":

| Case | Year | What the FTC alleged | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Automators AI | 2023–24 | AI-boosted online stores would deliver high earnings; more than $22M in consumer harm | Lifetime ban on selling e-commerce opportunities or coaching ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/automators)) |
| FBA Machine / Passive Scaling | 2024 | "Guaranteed income" turnkey stores | Permanent bar on selling business opportunities ([ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/antitrust_law/resources/newsletters/ftc-continues-crackdown-ai-deceit-e-commerce/)) |
| Ascend Ecom, Ecommerce Empire Builders | 2024 | AI-powered stores would earn thousands to $10,000 a month (part of "Operation AI Comply") | ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20241015-the-ftc-cracks-down-on-unfair-and-deceptive-practices-involving-the-use-of-ai)) |
| Click Profit | 2025 | AI "passive income" stores and fake Nike and Disney affiliations | Bars on business-opportunity sales and false earnings, affiliation and AI claims ([Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/33007-federal-trade-commission-issued-proposed-orders-against-click-profit-for-alleged-misrepresentation-of-online-sales-opportunities)) |

The closest analog to Meddy's concept is **Air AI**, a B2B "AI that grows your business" seller. It told small businesses they could earn back their investment quickly and promised refunds if they didn't earn two to three times their outlay. It rarely honored those refunds, and some buyers lost up to $250,000. The FTC charged it under Section 5, the Telemarketing Sales Rule and the Business Opportunity Rule. In March 2026 it settled with a **ban on marketing business opportunities and an $18M judgment**, largely suspended ([FTC, Mar 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/air-ai-its-owners-will-be-banned-marketing-business-opportunities-settle-ftc-charges-company-misled)). The lesson is that selling to small businesses rather than consumers is no safe harbor.

The federal Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR 437) requires a reasonable basis and written substantiation for any earnings claim at the moment it is made ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-437)). If the rule applies, the seller must also hand over a disclosure document. Our reading, based on the rule's structure and not legal advice, is that promising to supply customers to someone starting a new business, for a required payment, plausibly brings a seller under the rule. A January 2025 FTC proposal would extend coverage to "money-making opportunities" such as business coaching and add a new Earnings Claim Rule. No final rule could be confirmed as of October 2026 ([FTC NPRM](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/r111003earningclaimsnprm01132025.pdf)).

Virginia raises the stakes for a Sterling-based company. Its Business Opportunity Sales Act applies where a buyer pays **more than $500** and the seller says it will provide a sales or marketing program that lets the buyer earn more than they paid ([Va. Code §59.1-263](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/59.1-263)). A covered sale requires a written disclosure statement, possibly a bond or escrow, a written contract, and private remedies for buyers ([LIS](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodepopularnames/business-opportunity-sales-act/)). A $1,500 setup fee sold with "we'll turn your idea into a business that makes money" comes uncomfortably close to that definition.

Several other legal rules also shape the product design:

- **Ad content liability.** The platform will be treated as the speaker for ads and sites it generates. Section 230 does not clearly protect content a platform generates "in whole or in part" ([CDT](https://cdt.org/insights/section-230-and-its-applicability-to-generative-ai-a-legal-analysis/)).
- **Fake reviews.** The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule carries penalties of up to **$53,088 per violation**, and warning-letter enforcement began in December 2025 ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-signals-heightened-enforcement-of-new)). An AI that writes testimonials is a liability.
- **Copyright.** Purely AI-generated logos and copy are not copyrightable after the Supreme Court denied cert in *Thaler v. Perlmutter* in March 2026 ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-authorship-case)). "You own your brand" needs a human-refinement step to hold up.
- **Virginia contractor licensing.** A license is required for contracting work of $1,000 or more, and unlicensed contracting is a Class 1 misdemeanor ([Va. Code Title 54.1 Ch. 11](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title54.1/chapter11/)). Running ads for unlicensed contractors is a direct exposure, so verify DPOR licenses at onboarding.
- **Google Advanced Verification.** US locksmiths and garage-door services must pass it, which can take months ([Google](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7167635)).
- **Texting consent.** The FCC's one-to-one consent rule was vacated in January 2025 ([Morrison Foerster](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule)), but prior express written consent is still the safe standard for marketing texts and AI-voice follow-up.
- **Tracking pixels.** More than 4,000 California wiretap suits have been filed over website tracking since 2022 ([Concord](https://www.concord.tech/blog/california-wiretapping-claims-website-tracking)), so template sites should fire pixels only after consent.

## The money is with operators who have budgets, not idea-stage founders

Idea-stage users are a funnel, not a revenue base. In UENI's August 2026 onboarding data, which is not a representative sample, **about 90% of 7,413 new business owners planned to spend under $200/mo on marketing, and 26% planned $0** ([UENI](https://ueni.com/blog/small-business-marketing-budget/)). About **20–22% of new establishments close within their first year** ([SCORE](https://www.score.org/greaterphoenix/resource/blog-post/small-business-failure-rates-2024-summary); [startbusinessbystate](https://startbusinessbystate.com/business-survival-rates-by-state)). Outsourced marketing also churns fast when results lag: **67% of SMBs that outsourced marketing switched providers within six months**, mostly for lack of revenue results ([vcita](https://www.vcita.com/press/smb-survey-highlights-marketing-agency-churn)).

Home services is the opposite case: costs are knowable and tickets are large.

| Trade | Google Search CPL | Google LSA CPL | Average job value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plumbing | ~$76 | ~$57–69 | ~$2,208 |
| HVAC | ~$85 | ~$51 | ~$1,205 repair to ~$3,225 heating |
| Roofing | ~$228 | (sources conflict) | ~$12,000–18,000 |
| Electrical | — | ~$39–49 | — |

Sources: LocaliQ's 2025 search benchmarks put the home-services average CPL at about **$91** ([foundrycro](https://foundrycro.com/blog/home-services-marketing-benchmarks-by-trade/)). The LSA figures come from a February 2026 study of 888 contractors and a May 2026 snapshot ([enrichlabs](https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/google-local-service-ads-complete-guide-2026)). Meta home-improvement lead campaigns averaged **$41 per lead** ([theadspend](https://theadspend.com/benchmarks/facebook-leads-home-improvement)). Job values are from Housecall Pro and SearchLight data ([pipelineon](https://pipelineon.com/blog/hvac-plumbing-roofing-pricing-strategy/)). HVAC lead-to-job conversion runs **30–40%**, and call book rates range from under 50% to over 85% ([ainora](https://ainora.lt/blog/hvac-service-call-statistics-2026)).

The implied cost per booked job is roughly **$190 for plumbing and $240 for HVAC**, against tickets ten times larger. That margin supports a real management fee. It also shows where most of the leverage sits: in answering and booking the lead, not in generating it. The gap between a 50% and an 85% book rate is worth more than any creative tweak.

On cost of goods, AI generation is cheap and human time is what costs money. Our estimate from 2026 list prices is that a full brand kit, site copy, about 40 images and three 15-second video ads costs about **$15–45 per launch before regenerations**. That uses image prices of about $0.04 each and Veo 3.1 video at $0.20–0.75 per second ([pricepertoken](https://pricepertoken.com/gpt-image-pricing); [apiframe](https://apiframe.ai/blog/ai-video-api-pricing-2026)). Model churn is real: OpenAI said it would remove the Sora 2 API on September 24, 2026, and Veo 3 was retired in June ([apiframe](https://apiframe.ai/blog/ai-video-api-pricing-2026)). The generation layer should therefore be model-agnostic.

Pricing benchmarks in market:

| Model | Typical price |
|---|---|
| Ad management fee | 10–20% of spend; small local accounts usually on flat retainers of about $750–1,500/mo ([AgencyAnalytics](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/ppc-pricing); [clicksgeek](https://clicksgeek.com/ppc-management-services-cost/)) |
| Angi | About $15–85 per shared lead plus an annual fee ([Valley Marketing Group](https://thevalleymarketinggroup.com/blog/angi-vs-thumbtack-vs-google-lsa-contractors-2026/)) |
| Venture studios | Average equity take of about 34% ([ninetwothree](https://ninetwothree.co/blog/how-do-venture-studios-make-money)) |

Retention data also favors a bundle over a single product. Vendasta's study of 100,000+ SMB accounts found that customers sold one product retain about 30% at two years, and that regular proof-of-performance reporting **lifts retention 38%** ([Vendasta](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/vendasta-client-churn-study/)). Vendasta sells a bundling platform, so read that number with some skepticism.

As illustrative LTV math: at $500/mo revenue, 70% gross margin and 5% monthly churn, LTV is about $7,000. That supports roughly $1,500–2,300 of acquisition cost for an existing operator. A $20–50/mo idea-stage subscriber can only be acquired free or virally.

## Distribution comes from instant demos, resellers and the moment of formation

The growth stories in this market share three mechanics:

- **An instant free "aha."** Durable created **26,000 sites in its first month and about 6 million within a year** from a free button that generates a site in roughly 30 seconds ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/?p=367519)).
- **Recurring reseller economics.** GoHighLevel pays a **40% recurring affiliate commission** and lets agencies white-label it, which turns agencies into its sales force ([thestackinsiders](https://www.thestackinsiders.com/blog/gohighlevel-affiliate-program)).
- **Formation-moment partnerships.** LegalZoom partnered with Wix to give newly formed businesses a website at incorporation ([Artificial Lawyer](https://www.artificiallawyer.com/2022/05/03/legalzoom-website-builder-wix-form-partnership/)), and Stripe Atlas runs a perks marketplace open to partner applications ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/partner-benefits)).

Formation-moment channels reach idea-stage founders with low budgets, so they feed a low-priced tier. For established home-services operators, the research found no direct evidence for specific channels. Trade-supply houses, OEM dealer networks, field-service software marketplaces and bookkeeper or consultant referrals are reasonable hypotheses to test, not proven paths. Given the six-month churn data, the most persuasive sales asset will be a documented case study with before-and-after cost per lead and booked jobs. NovaProHome can supply exactly that.

## Our recommendation (opinion, not sourced findings)

*Everything in this section is our judgment, built on the findings above. Validate the legal points with a Virginia attorney before launch.*

**Positioning.** Position the company as "the launch-and-growth system for independent tradespeople." The promise is: we build your brand, website, ads and lead follow-up, and show you every lead and booked job. Keep "idea-to-business" as the vision on the About page and in investor conversations, not in the headline or the sales pitch. Use a name and voice that sound like an operator's tool, not a get-rich product. That framing avoids business-opportunity language, separates the company from GoDaddy Airo and Durable, and draws on Meddy's real advantage: he has done this end to end for a home-services business and has spent a career managing field sales teams.

**Wedge.** The first customer is the licensed HVAC, plumbing or electrical tech in the DMV who is going independent or running a one-to-five-truck shop and is tired of Angi. These buyers sit between "idea" and "operating business." They have a skill, a license that can be verified, real demand, and tickets that make a $1,500–$3,000 monthly ad budget rational. They are also the buyers most likely to see a cost per booked job they can trust within 60 days. Avoid locksmith and garage-door businesses (Advanced Verification delays), roofing at first (high CPL, long sales cycle), and anything touching financing, housing or health.

**MVP scope.** Build only the closed loop. The pieces are:

- An AI brand and one-page site generator (name, logo, colors, copy, service-area pages) with trademark and domain knockout checks.
- Google Search plus LSA management and Meta lead campaigns, run on customer-owned accounts through Meddy's MCC and Business Manager.
- Call tracking with dynamic numbers and recording.
- Instant SMS response within 60 seconds and missed-call text-back, with logged written consent and A2P registration handled for the customer.
- Offline conversion upload of booked jobs back to Google and Meta.
- A weekly report built around one number: cost per booked job.

Integrate with Jobber or Housecall Pro rather than building a CRM. Do not generate custom software, mobile apps or vibe-coded tools in the MVP. TikTok, YouTube, Microsoft and Nextdoor come later. Ops can be human-in-the-loop behind a simple internal dashboard. The software should automate Meddy's own workflow first and become self-serve second.

**Pricing.** Use three tiers:

| Tier | Who | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Free generator | Anyone with an idea | Free brand kit and site preview |
| Launch Kit | Idea-stage self-serve users | About $39–49/mo: custom domain, site, Google Business Profile setup checklist, lead inbox |
| Launch & Run | Licensed operators | About $1,500 one-time setup plus the greater of $750/mo or 15% of ad spend, with a $1,500/mo minimum ad budget paid on the customer's own card |

For Launch & Run, no long contracts: month-to-month after a 90-day initial term, with a written, simple, honored cancellation policy. After about 10 accounts have produced real cost-per-booked-job data, test a per-booked-job price (for example, a flat fee per verified booked job) with a few customers. That would be the strongest anti-Angi message. Skip equity and revenue share: they are unauditable for cash trades and add business-opportunity optics. Because the setup fee exceeds Virginia's $500 threshold, get a written counsel opinion on the Business Opportunity Sales Act before charging it.

**First 90 days.**

| Window | Work |
|---|---|
| Days 1–30 | Form the entity. Get the counsel opinion on the FTC Business Opportunity Rule and Virginia law, plus E&O, media-liability and cyber insurance quotes. Start Google brand verification and Basic API access, Meta Business Verification and App Review, and the GBP API application. Turn NovaProHome into a numbers-backed case study. Recruit five design-partner tradespeople through Meddy's local network at a discounted setup fee. |
| Days 31–60 | Run those five accounts mostly by hand with internal tooling. Stand up call tracking, SMS speed-to-lead, Meta CAPI and Google offline conversion upload. Verify DPOR licenses. Ramp Meta spend gradually inside new-account limits. Record hours spent per account per week, because human time is the real cost of goods. |
| Days 61–90 | Publish the free generator as a lead magnet. Publish anonymized cost-per-booked-job results, as typical results with substantiation on file. Fix pricing from actual hours and margins. Pitch two or three local supply houses or a field-service software partner. Go or no-go: if design partners are not seeing a cost per booked job below roughly 15% of average ticket by day 90, fix the follow-up loop before adding customers. |

**Phased roadmap.**

| Phase | Timing | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Months 0–6 | 10–25 DMV home-services accounts, a tech-enabled service, Google, LSA and Meta only. Goal: proof and margin. |
| Phase 2 | Months 6–18 | Self-serve onboarding for the same trades nationally. Microsoft mirroring. Nextdoor partnership. AI-voice answering. A referral program for bookkeepers and consultants at around 20–30% recurring commission. A first adjacent vertical with recurring tickets, such as cleaning or lawn care. |
| Phase 3 | 18+ months | Broaden toward the idea-to-business vision. Add TikTok and YouTube (via Demand Gen) once creative and conversion data justify them. Possibly a white-label agency tier. Possibly a "distribution layer" for vibe-coded apps from Lovable and Replit users, which is the second unserved gap. |

**Risks to avoid.** Do not front ad spend or put customers on a shared payment method. Do not recreate ad accounts for customers who have been suspended, since that is "circumventing systems." Do not let one bad customer taint the MCC: screen customers (identity, license, vertical) before linking accounts. Do not auto-generate reviews, testimonials, "licensed and insured" claims or statistics the customer has not attested to. Keep an approval log for every ad. Do not use outbound cold-call scripts with result promises. Do not build on a single generative model. Do not let the free tier's compute costs grow faster than paid conversions, which is the Wix lesson.

**What not to promise.** Never say or imply:

- "a business that makes money"
- "passive income"
- "AI runs it for you"
- any dollar-per-month or ROI figure
- "guaranteed leads"
- a refund conditioned on earnings
- that customers "own" an AI-only logo outright

Never present the generator's output as legal trademark clearance. Promise only what the business controls: speed of build, transparent reporting, response time to leads, and month-to-month terms.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the opportunity. The scarce asset in 2026 is no longer the ability to generate a brand, a site or an ad. GoDaddy, Wix, Lovable, Meta and Google are racing each other to make those free. What is scarce is verified outcome data: which calls became jobs, at what cost, fed back into ad optimization. A company that owns that loop for a narrow trade gets better targeting, a stronger case study and lower churn with every account, while a broad "AI builds your business" tool gets more compute costs and more FTC exposure.

Meddy's background as a multi-unit sales leader who has already run the NovaProHome playbook by hand is a good fit for the operator-heavy, trust-based first phase. That phase decides whether the broader vision ever earns the right to exist. The open questions to resolve with real data in the first 90 days are human hours per account, true cost per booked job in the DMV (likely above national averages, though unverified), and whether Virginia counsel signs off on the setup-fee structure.
