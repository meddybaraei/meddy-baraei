## 0. REVISED: licensing first (compliance risk today)
- Va. Code § 9.1-138 defines "electronic security equipment" as (i) alarm signaling devices, including burglar alarms, and (ii) "cameras used to detect intrusions, concealment or theft, to safeguard and protect persons and property". An "electronic security business" is anyone who installs, services or maintains that equipment *for an end user*. Sources: law.lis.virginia.gov § 9.1-138; Justia/FindLaw copies.
- Gemini's reading of § 9.1-140: there is no exception for installers working for hire, and a DPOR license does not replace DCJS. The only near fit is "licensed professional engineers … acting in their professional capacity", which needs a PE license, not a BS. Operating without a license is a Class 1 misdemeanor (§ 9.1-147). *Gemini's summary, not yet confirmed by DCJS. Our DCJS question from Oct 26 is still open.*
- In plain terms: **paid installs of security cameras and alarms need a DCJS electronic security business license, a compliance agent and registered technicians.** Smart locks, thermostats, Wi-Fi/mesh, lighting and EV are not in that definition. Doorbell cameras are a gray area; treat them as covered to be safe.
- DCJS steps, as Gemini reported them (verify on dcjs.virginia.gov before paying):
  - 14-hour entry-level technician training (30E core + 35E technician)
  - Compliance-agent designation
  - Fingerprints (about $50 per person)
  - Business license (about $800 + $50 category fee, for 2 years)
  - Technician registration (about $25)
  - Proof of GL insurance (we have $2M)
  - Typical time: about 30–60 days
- Maryland: separate MSP Security Systems Agency license plus technician registration. Allow up to about 90 business days.
- **Until licensed:** lead with the services outside the definition (Wi-Fi/mesh, smart locks, thermostats, lighting, TV/network), and get licensed as fast as possible. Meddy decides how to handle security jobs already booked. Calling DCJS directly is the fastest way to a definite answer.

## 6. Selling on calls and site visits: CLOSER
1. **C**larify why they're here: "What made you reach out now?"
2. **L**abel the problem: "So right now X, you want Y, and Z is in the way?"
3. **O**verview past pain: "What have you tried? How did that go?" (DIY, a handyman, an app that never worked)
4. **S**ell the vacation: what life looks like after. "Your phone shows who's at the door, packages are on camera, nothing to manage."
5. **E**xplain away concerns: time ("one visit"), money (fixed written price, downsell), fit ("works with what you have"), past bad installers ("engineer-run, clean work, guaranteed")
6. **R**einforce the decision: confirm the date, send a written summary and what to expect, then follow up after the job and ask for a review.
